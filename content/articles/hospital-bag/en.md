# Your hospital bag starts with the hospital's own list

Search "hospital bag Japan" and you will find dozens of long lists. The most reliable one, though, is **the packing list your own hospital gives you** once your delivery booking is confirmed. Japanese maternity hospitals differ a lot in what they provide (postpartum underwear and pads, baby clothes, nappies) and what you must bring.

## Steps

1. When your booking is confirmed, ask for the admission guide (入院のご案内). Many hospitals publish it on their website, in Japanese.
2. Mark what the hospital provides. Do not buy those.
3. Sort what is left into "during the stay", "for going home" and "for the baby".
4. Pack it by about week 34 and keep it near the door.

## Often missing from the hospital's list

- **Documents**: the Mother-and-Child Health Handbook, your health-insurance card (or My Number card used as one), the hospital's patient card, your copy of the direct-payment agreement for the lump-sum allowance, and a personal seal (inkan) if the hospital asks for one. The Navi's "This week" screen lists these too.
- **A long charging cable**: sockets are often far from the bed.
- **A bottle with a straw**: you can drink lying down during labour and after.
- **Going-home clothes for the baby and a child car seat**: if you leave by car, a child seat is required by law.

## Things you usually do not need to buy

- Anything the hospital says it provides.
- Items listed only as "nice to have". You can buy them after you are home, once you know you need them.
