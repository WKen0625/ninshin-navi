# How Tsugiraku works, and what we promise

From the day you learn you are pregnant until a few weeks after the birth, there are more than ten pieces of paperwork in Japan: the pregnancy notification, the Mother-and-Child Health Handbook (母子手帳), prenatal check-up coupons, the hospital booking, the childbirth lump-sum allowance, the birth registration, the child allowance, health-insurance enrolment, and the subsidies your ward offers. Each has its own counter and deadline, and the details differ from ward to ward.

Tsugiraku's **Pregnancy Paperwork Navi** replaces the scattered searching with **one thing: the single next step that applies to you right now.**

## You enter four things

1. The ward you live in (currently the 23 wards of Tokyo)
2. Your due date (and, if you know it, the date the heartbeat was confirmed)
3. Your preferences (whether you are considering an epidural, and how far you are willing to travel)
4. The papers you already hold (handbook, coupons, hospital receipt… or "nothing yet")

No name, no e-mail address, no phone number. Everything you enter stays in your own browser.

## You get three screens

- **This week**: your procedures, nearest deadline first. The top item is highlighted as your Next Action, with the reason it is on top. Tick it off and the next one appears.
- **Hospitals and deadlines**: every facility in your ward where you can give birth. The order is simply "earliest published booking-deadline week first". It is not a ranking. Enter your postal code and you also see a rough distance from home.
- **Money**: the facility's typical fee − the ¥500,000 childbirth lump sum − Tokyo and ward subsidies = your estimated out-of-pocket cost, with application deadlines.

The Navi itself is currently in Japanese. This article and the others marked "English" are here to help you understand what it does before you start.

## What we promise

- **Every figure has a source and a check date.** Each number links to the official page of the ward, Tokyo or the national government, and shows when we last checked it. Information without a source cannot be published — the system refuses it.
- **No advertising on the hospital screen.** We accept no money from hospitals. Products appear only in articles, and those articles say "PR" at the top.
- **No medical advice.** We cover paperwork, deadlines and money only. For anything about your health or treatment, talk to your clinic.
- **Minimal personal data.** No names, no check-up results. Only if you opt in to the anonymous "record" survey do we store your answer on our server, for aggregate statistics.

## How we keep it accurate

Once a week we automatically watch every official page we cite. If one changes, a person reads the change before we update anything. Until then, the screen says "under review".

If you spot a mistake, use "report an error" on any screen. No name or e-mail needed.
