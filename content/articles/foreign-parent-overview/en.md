# Foreign parents in Tokyo: the big picture after your baby is born

This guide lists what you need to do **in addition to** the Japanese birth registration when one or both parents are not Japanese nationals, with the order and the deadlines. The Japanese version is at [外国籍の親のための全体像](/articles/foreign-parent-overview). The Navi itself is in Japanese for now; the [English guide](/en) explains how to use it with a translation tool or a Japanese-speaking partner.

## The big picture: three things beyond the birth registration

Every child born in Japan is registered at the ward office, whatever the parents' nationality. The Ministry of Justice page says this must be done within 14 days of the birth. Foreign parents usually need three more things:

1. A residence status for the child (if the child is a foreign national): Immigration Services Agency, within 30 days of birth
2. Registering the birth at your country's embassy or consulate, and a passport for the child
3. Health insurance for the child (as a dependent on an employer plan, or National Health Insurance)

Which of these apply depends on the child's nationality. If one parent is Japanese, the child normally has Japanese nationality and step 1 is not needed. If both parents are foreign nationals, the child is also a foreign national and step 1 is required. Nationality rules differ by country, so check with your embassy.

## 1. Residence status for the child: within 30 days of birth (both parents foreign)

A foreign child born in Japan has no residence status at birth. The Immigration Services Agency page on the "Application for Permission to Acquire Status of Residence" says:

- Who: a foreign national who came to reside in Japan without going through landing procedures (for example by birth) and who intends to stay **more than 60 days** from that day
- When: within **30 days** of the day the reason arose (the date of birth)
- Where: the regional immigration office with jurisdiction over your place of residence
- Fee: none
- Documents: depend on the residence status the child will receive, which follows the parents' status

The birth registration (14 days) and the residence application (30 days) go to **different offices with different deadlines**. The usual order is to file the birth registration first, get a certificate of acceptance and a copy of the resident record from the ward, then go to immigration with those, the parents' residence cards, the maternal and child health handbook, and passports. Check the latest document list on the immigration page.

## 2. "Reservation of nationality" is for births outside Japan. Not needed in Tokyo

You may see the term 国籍留保 (reservation of Japanese nationality) and worry. It applies only to a child **with Japanese nationality who is born outside Japan**. The Ministry of Justice Q&A explains that when such a child also acquires another nationality at birth, the parents must declare the reservation together with the birth registration, or the child loses Japanese nationality retroactively. The declaration is written in the "other" field of the birth form, and the deadline for a birth abroad is 3 months.

A child born in Tokyo has nothing to reserve. If the child has Japanese nationality, the birth registration is enough.

## 3. Registering the birth at your embassy, and the child's passport

The Japanese birth registration is a record for Japanese authorities only. To have your home country recognise the birth, you register it separately at your embassy or consulate. The method, the documents (a Japanese certificate of acceptance of the birth registration and a translation are often required), the deadline, and the passport application all differ by country, so check your embassy's website.

The child's residence application may be accepted even if the passport is not ready, but the child cannot travel without one. If you plan to visit your home country, start the embassy registration and passport early.

## 4. The child's name on the birth form: katakana, with Roman letters below

The name field on the Japanese birth form is written in Japanese. Chuo City's page says that for a child of foreign nationality, you write the name in katakana in the name field and the Roman-letter spelling below it; nationals of countries that use Chinese characters may write the name in kanji.

Things to decide before you file:

- The resident record will show the katakana (or kanji) name. The residence card will use the Roman-letter spelling from the passport. **Two spellings will exist on different documents**, so agree the katakana version as a family first, and expect nurseries, hospitals and banks to ask which one to register.
- Where a middle name goes, and how to split family and given names, can be discussed at the ward counter before filing.
- Wards differ slightly in their guidance. Check the birth registration page of your own ward.

## 5. No health insurance, no childbirth lump sum

The lump-sum allowance for childbirth (in principle 500,000 yen per child) is paid by health insurance. The Japan Health Insurance Association page says it is paid when an **insured person or their dependent** gives birth. If you are in neither an employer plan nor National Health Insurance, there is no lump sum.

- Employees in an employer plan: the birth of the insured person or of a dependent spouse is covered
- Self-employed people and others not in an employer plan: if you are in your ward's National Health Insurance, it pays the lump sum
- Short-term residents not in any Japanese health insurance: no lump sum, so you pay the full cost of the birth yourself

Check which insurance you are in as soon as the pregnancy is confirmed (your insurance card or My Number card). After the birth, add the child to health insurance too (through the employer for an employer plan, at the ward office for National Health Insurance). The ward's medical subsidy for children also requires the child to be insured.

## Multilingual handbooks and interpreters: ask your ward

Some wards hand out multilingual versions of the maternal and child health handbook, and some have interpreters or translation devices at the counter. Which languages, and whether you need to book, differ by ward and cannot be confirmed here, so ask your ward's child-rearing counter.

## In time order

1. When you file the pregnancy notification: check your health insurance; ask your embassy what it needs for a birth registration
2. Within 14 days of birth: birth registration at the ward office (decide the katakana spelling beforehand)
3. After that: get a certificate of acceptance and a resident record; add the child to health insurance
4. Within 30 days of birth: if the child is a foreign national, apply for residence status at immigration
5. In parallel: embassy birth registration and the child's passport
